HORIA: 4D Human-Object Reconstruction
via Temporally Grounded Novel-View Video Generation

Hyeongjin Nam, Taeryung Lee, Daniel Sungho Jung, Kyoung Mu Lee
Seoul National University
arXiv Paper Video

TL;DR. Given a monocular video, HORIA reconstructs temporally coherent 4D human-object interactions by decomposing the video into grounded sub-actions and generating interaction-aware novel-view supervision.

Abstract

Joint 4D reconstruction of a human and object from a monocular video is essential for understanding how people interact with their environment. Despite recent progress, this task remains challenging for two main reasons. First, an interaction video often contains multiple sub-actions, making it difficult for a single global description to identify the interaction state at each moment. Second, severe human-object occlusion in a single-view video provides insufficient geometric evidence and often leads to inconsistent reconstructions.

To address these challenges, we introduce HORIA, a framework built upon two core designs. First, motion-aware video captioning decomposes the input into temporally grounded, semantically distinct sub-actions and assigns a specific text description to each interaction phase. Second, an interaction-aware re-rendering pipeline renders the initial 4D human and object from novel viewpoints and refines those renderings with a text-guided video diffusion prior. The resulting novel-view videos provide auxiliary supervision that enables accurate, geometrically consistent, and temporally coherent 4D reconstruction across diverse interaction scenarios.

Pipeline Details

Given a monocular interaction video, HORIA first estimates the human and object motion and decomposes the sequence into temporally grounded sub-actions with motion-aware captions. For each sub-action, it renders the initial 4D reconstruction from novel viewpoints and refines the videos using semantic and contact-aware text guidance. These refined multi-view observations supervise a joint optimization of the human and object, improving spatial accuracy and temporal consistency under severe occlusion.

Video Decomposition

HORIA tracks changes in the initial object motion to identify coarse interaction boundaries, then uses motion-aware captioning and temporal grounding to divide the video into semantically distinct sub-actions with precise time spans.

Interaction-Aware Re-Rendering

HORIA renders the initial human-object reconstruction from novel viewpoints and refines each video with a text-to-video diffusion prior and region-specific prompts, providing reliable multi-view supervision for 4D optimization.
